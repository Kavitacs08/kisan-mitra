Disorder: Blossom End Rot
Crop: Tomato
Region: Karnataka, India

Sec. 6.1 — Visual Symptoms
A sunken, dark brown to black leathery patch at the blossom end (bottom tip) of
the fruit, opposite the stem. The rest of the fruit usually ripens normally
around it. This is a physiological disorder, not a disease — there is no fungal
growth, insect damage, or spreading lesion pattern on leaves.

Sec. 6.2 — Triggering Conditions
Caused by localized calcium deficiency in the developing fruit, almost always
driven by inconsistent watering (alternating drought and flooding) rather than
true soil calcium shortage, since uneven water supply disrupts calcium uptake
and transport. Fast-growing young plants under high fruit load are most
susceptible, especially during hot, dry spells.

Sec. 6.3 — Recommended Treatment
There is no treatment for already-affected fruit; remove and discard it.
Prevent further occurrence by maintaining even soil moisture through mulching
and consistent, scheduled watering rather than infrequent heavy watering.
A foliar calcium spray can help in acute cases but even watering is the more
effective long-term fix. Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilizer, which accelerates
leafy growth faster than calcium can be transported to fruit.
