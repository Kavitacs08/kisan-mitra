Disease: Bacterial Wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum)
Crop: Tomato
Region: Karnataka, India

Sec. 4.1 — Visual Symptoms
Sudden, irreversible wilting of the whole plant or individual branches, often with
leaves still green at the time of collapse (unlike drought wilting, which shows
leaf yellowing first). Cutting the lower stem reveals brown, discolored vascular
tissue, and a milky bacterial ooze may appear when the cut stem is pressed or
suspended in water.

Sec. 4.2 — Triggering Conditions
Favored by warm soil temperatures (above 24°C) and high soil moisture. The
bacterium survives for years in soil and spreads through irrigation water, soil
movement, and infected transplants. Root wounds from transplanting or cultivation
increase infection risk.

Sec. 4.3 — Recommended Treatment
No effective chemical cure exists once a plant is infected; remove and destroy
wilted plants immediately, including the root ball, to limit soil contamination.
Avoid planting tomato, potato, brinjal, or chili in the same soil for at least
2-3 seasons. Improve field drainage to reduce waterlogging. Source certified
disease-free transplants and avoid transplanting during waterlogged conditions.
