Disease: Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)
Crop: Tomato
Region: Karnataka, India

Sec. 2.1 — Visual Symptoms
Water-soaked, pale green to dark olive-brown blotches on leaves and stems that
expand rapidly, often with a white, fuzzy fungal growth visible on the underside
of leaves in humid conditions. Unlike early blight, lesions lack concentric rings
and can appear on young upper leaves too. Fruit develops firm, dark, greasy-looking
brown patches. Entire plants can collapse within days under cool, wet weather.

Sec. 2.2 — Triggering Conditions
Favored by cool temperatures (15-20°C) with high humidity or prolonged leaf
wetness, typically after extended rain or heavy fog. Spreads explosively through
wind-blown spores during cool, overcast, damp spells. Far faster-moving and more
destructive than early blight.

Sec. 2.3 — Recommended Treatment
Act immediately: remove and destroy infected plant material, do not compost it.
Apply a mancozeb 75% WP or copper-based fungicide preventively before forecast rain,
and continue at 5-7 day intervals during cool, wet spells. If an entire plot is
heavily infected, prioritize protecting unaffected neighboring plots over trying to
save badly collapsed plants. Avoid working in wet fields to prevent spreading spores
on tools and clothing.
