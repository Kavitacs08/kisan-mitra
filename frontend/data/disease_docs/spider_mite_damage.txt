Pest Damage: Spider Mite (Tetranychus urticae)
Crop: Tomato
Region: Karnataka, India

Sec. 8.1 — Visual Symptoms
Fine, pale yellow or bronze stippling/speckling on leaves, starting on the
underside and often most visible when holding the leaf up to light. Fine
silk webbing may be visible between leaves and stems in heavy infestations.
Unlike fungal spotting, there are no distinct lesion borders — damage looks
like a diffuse dusty discoloration, and leaves eventually turn bronze and dry.

Sec. 8.2 — Triggering Conditions
Favored by hot, dry weather and dusty field conditions — the opposite of the
humid conditions that favor most fungal diseases. Water-stressed plants are
more vulnerable. Populations explode fast in dry spells and can be worsened by
overuse of certain broad-spectrum insecticides that also kill the mites'
natural predators.

Sec. 8.3 — Recommended Treatment
Rinse plants with a strong jet of water to physically dislodge mites in mild
cases. For heavier infestations, apply a miticide or a neem-oil-based spray,
focusing coverage on the underside of leaves where mites concentrate. Avoid
dusty, drought-stressed conditions where possible by maintaining consistent
irrigation. Avoid broad-spectrum insecticide overuse, which removes the
mites' natural predators and can make outbreaks worse.
