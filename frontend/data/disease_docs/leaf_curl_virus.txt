Disease: Tomato Leaf Curl Virus (ToLCV)
Crop: Tomato
Region: Karnataka, India

Sec. 3.1 — Visual Symptoms
Upward curling and crinkling of leaflets, leaves appear smaller and thickened,
overall plant growth is stunted and bushy ("bonsai" appearance). Yellowing may
appear along leaf veins. Flowering and fruit set are severely reduced on infected
plants; fruit already set is usually unaffected in size but overall yield drops
sharply.

Sec. 3.2 — Triggering Conditions
Transmitted exclusively by whitefly (Bemisia tabaci), not by soil, water, or
mechanical contact. Risk rises sharply in warm, dry weather that favors whitefly
population growth, and near other infected solanaceous crops or weeds acting as a
virus reservoir. Young seedlings infected early in the season suffer the most yield
loss.

Sec. 3.3 — Recommended Treatment
There is no curative chemical treatment for the virus itself — management is
whitefly control and prevention. Apply imidacloprid 17.8% SL as a soil drench or
foliar spray to control whitefly populations, rotating with a different
insecticide class every 2-3 applications to avoid resistance. Remove and destroy
infected plants promptly to reduce the whitefly's source of new virus. Use yellow
sticky traps to monitor whitefly pressure and consider reflective mulch for young
transplants in high-risk periods.
