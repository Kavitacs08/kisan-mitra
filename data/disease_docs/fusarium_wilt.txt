Disease: Fusarium Wilt (Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. lycopersici)
Crop: Tomato
Region: Karnataka, India

Sec. 5.1 — Visual Symptoms
Yellowing and wilting that typically begins on one side of the plant or on lower
leaves first, progressing upward over days to weeks. Vascular tissue inside the
stem shows a brown discoloration when cut, similar to bacterial wilt, but
Fusarium progresses more slowly and the plant does not ooze bacterial slime.

Sec. 5.2 — Triggering Conditions
Favored by warm soil temperatures (around 28°C), acidic soil, and soil already
weakened by nematode damage or nitrogen imbalance. The fungus survives for many
years in soil as resistant spores and spreads through infected soil, water, and
tools.

Sec. 5.3 — Recommended Treatment
No chemical cure after infection; remove and destroy infected plants and avoid
replanting tomato in the same soil for 4+ years if possible. Favor resistant or
tolerant tomato varieties where available. Improve soil pH toward neutral and
manage nematodes, since nematode wounds increase infection risk. Solarizing soil
with clear plastic sheeting during the hottest months can reduce fungal spore
load before the next planting.
